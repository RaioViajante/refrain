# Evidence: M3.4, the definitive public Home

**What this is.** A record of what M3.4 built, how it was checked, and what was not. It describes the branch `feat/m3-4-public-home` (issue #42, pull request #43), started from `main` at `d48711b8fede3031a1e4b6424747f81cd6a39e5a` (M3.3 merged). Every test ran against the **disposable test database**; the owner's development database and development server were not touched, and no backup was made or restored. Everything shown is synthetic: public catalog facts (Grace, Pink Moon, Twin Fantasy, Cleopatra and their artists) with writing labelled `[Test]`, and a generated image standing in for a cover. **Nothing is deployed, the public-reads switch is on only in the tests' own servers, and gates G1 to G8 stay open.**

## Baseline

- `main` and `origin/main` were both `d48711b8fede3031a1e4b6424747f81cd6a39e5a`, and the working tree was clean.
- An owner's `next dev` was running on port 3000 against the development database. It was identified and left alone; no test or screenshot used it. Next.js restarted its worker when `next.config.ts` changed, which is its own behavior.
- The placeholder at `/` said "Development build. The approved homepage is not built yet." and imported nothing. The frame linked Home and showed Albums as the current page on the album journal. The not-found view's "Back to the start" led to the placeholder.
- **The character.** `reference/refrain-home-character.png`: 1536×1024, 8-bit RGBA, not interlaced, 1,949,226 bytes, SHA-256 `ad58315e…ba63c`, byte-identical to `reference/refrain/pages/refrain-character.png` (which the V4.4 pages load) and to `public/artwork/refrain-home-character.png`. It carries a 21,881-byte C2PA chunk (`caBX`). 68.5% of its pixels are fully transparent, none fully opaque (the drawing's darkest alpha is 254), and the corners are clear. It was read, never written; its hash is the same after the work.
- **The V4.4 Home** (`refrainV44-Home.dc.html`, `…HomeMobile`, and their captures) was read for its markup, CSS, and copy: the notebook layout (220px margin, 64px gap), the opening (character 380/300/280px, introduction 23/20.5px, "Most recent" 17px with the title in Kalam 21px), the rows (118px date, Kalam 22px title, 48px frame), the albums (four or two to a row, 40/28 and 30/16 gaps), and the section spacing (76px, then 96px). Its introduction, dates, memory titles, and artist lines are bracketed placeholders.

## Owner decisions (issue #42)

1. **The switch.** ADR 0006 D4, read literally, would make `/` the unknown-address `404` on every default deployment. Asked, the owner chose a Home that always answers `200`, whose listings are empty while reads are off, recorded as amendment A4, whose exact text the owner approved in the final review of pull request #43.
2. **The introduction.** The approved screen leaves it to the author. Asked, the owner chose a neutral interim line, not in the first person and with nothing biographical: "A personal music journal about albums, songs, and the memories attached to them." It is one constant, `HOME_INTRODUCTION`, and also the page's description.
3. **The production copy** of the character was authorized in the M3.4 brief ("If a production copy is needed, create an appropriately optimized derivative"). Serving 1.95 MB as the Home's largest image was the need.

## The Home as built

- **Order:** skip link, navigation (Home, current), the visually hidden `h1` "refrain", the character, the introduction, then "Most recent: <title> · Album journal · <date>" (or "Nothing published yet."), **Recently written** (up to five journals, most recently published first), **Albums** (up to four albums, each by its presented journal, featured first, most recently published first), then the footer's closing line.
- **Headings:** `h1` refrain, `h2` Recently written, `h2` Albums. Cards and rows are links whose names are their text (spaces between their parts, so a screen reader hears "Oct 20, 2026 Grace Album journal · Jeff Buckley"); artwork is decorative inside them; a rating is an image named "My rating: 4.5 out of 5".
- **Dates** are the publication date of the version a reader is shown (the reader's language, else the original, else the other). The date a piece was written is private editing metadata.
- **Not built, as the design system records:** the Artists and Memories sections, the "All …" links, the Albums, Artists, and Memories navigation, and the language selector, because their pages do not exist; memories and artist journals in "Recently written", because they cannot be published yet.
- **Metadata:** the title "refrain", the introduction as the description, a canonical address and `og:url` from the site's own origin (`BETTER_AUTH_URL`, read per request), `og:type` `website`, `og:site_name` `refrain`, `og:locale` `en_US`. No structured data and no Open Graph image: nothing on the page needs them, and a social image of the character would be a new derived use.
- **Sitemap:** the Home first, then the album journals, only while reads are on (unchanged otherwise).

The departures from the approved screen, each with its reason, are in [Design System § Public Home](../design-system.md#public-home-m34).

## The character

The Home serves `public/artwork/refrain-home-character.webp`, made with the approved 404 copy's recipe: `cwebp -q 90 -m 6 -sharp_yuv -alpha_q 100 -exact -metadata none` (libwebp 1.6.0). 312,852 bytes (16% of the original), SHA-256 `e98f523f…deec`, 1536×1024, chunks `VP8X`, `ALPH` (lossless), `VP8 `, no metadata.

Measured by decoding both files (the original's PNG filters undone in a short script, the copy through `dwebp -pam`), composited on white. The same script, run on the approved 404 copy, reproduced that copy's documented 42.4 dB and largest difference of 19, so its method matches the earlier review.

## Privacy and the switch

- **Reads.** Both lists start from the predicate fragment and from `servedAlbumJournalAddresses`, the same rule as the sitemap and `resolvePublicSlug`'s "found", so every link answers `200` and never a redirect. The presented journal is one SQL fragment shared with `resolvePresentedJournal`. The read returns slug, album title, credits, year, the journal's rating, the cover's image sources, and a publication date: no id, status, deletion state, count, unpublished language, or writing. A test searches the whole read for every entry, album, and artist id in the database and finds none.
- **The switch (A4).** The page checks it before reading, and the reading entry point checks it again. Off, the answer is the one for a site with nothing published. Five servers of one build (unset, `TRUE`, `1`, `true ` with a space, `true`):
  - with nothing published, the switch-off page equals the switch-on page as a reader receives it (status, every header, visible text, every tag with its attributes, and the metadata);
  - every switch-off server gives the same **whole** response, Flight data included, in GET, HEAD, RSC, router prefetch, two segment prefetches, `If-None-Match`, `If-Modified-Since`, `Range`, `Accept: application/json`, and with a cookie, and gives it again, unchanged, after journals in every state are seeded (the independent security review found the first version of this test compared GET only, and only the markup);
  - `PUT`, `PATCH`, `DELETE`, `OPTIONS`, and a body-less `POST` answer `405`. `/` is not in the proxy's matcher, so a `POST` that could be a Server Action reaches the framework: a form body renders the Home (`200`), a multipart body `400`, an unknown action `409`. Each is the same with the switch on or off and names no journal while it is off. The album addresses answer `405` to every `POST` from the proxy; adding `/` to the proxy only for this was judged a larger change to a security-critical file than the behavior warrants;
  - `/index`, `/index.html`, `/index.rsc`, and `/index.segments/…` answer the not-found `404`, the same either way.

  A mutation that removed both guards failed four of these tests.
- **Why not byte for byte.** Two requests to one server already differ in a random 22-character key that the framework writes into its Flight data, and (while the Home streamed, before the no-JavaScript fix) a streamed part could arrive at a different place in the document; so responses are compared after masking that key (`masked`, the album tests' helper) and as markup and text. In a browser, the empty Home and the switch-off Home are pixel-identical at 1440 and 390 px (the same SHA-256 for both screenshots).
- **States never listed:** draft, private, deleted, restored (a restore stays private), published then unpublished, a previous address, and an unpublished translation's date. Their titles and addresses are searched for in every response of the HTTP run, with the synthetic private markers.
- **A failing database,** with reads on: the Home shows "The latest writing could not be loaded just now." in place of the lists. Only the read is inside a `try`; `connection()`, which the framework uses to defer rendering, is not. Nothing is logged, and a test with an unreachable database and a unique password finds it neither in the page nor in the server log.
- **Headers.** `/` is `no-store` with the public CSP (covers from the archive only), `nosniff`, `strict-origin-when-cross-origin`, and `DENY` framing, in every form; no `Age`, `X-Cache`, `CF-Cache-Status`, `X-Vercel-Cache`, or `x-nextjs-cache` on the document. The framework adds `x-nextjs-prerender`, `x-nextjs-postponed`, and `x-nextjs-stale-time: 300`, as on the album journal: measured again after the no-JavaScript fix, unchanged; the last is the client router's hint for the segments it prefetches, which hold no data (the root layout's generic not-found fallback). Six responses in the request-form matrix carry `x-nextjs-cache`; each is empty or byte-identical to one of the Home's build files (`index.html`, `index.segments/*.rsc`), as A2 requires, and after every request no file under `.next` holds a seeded title or address.
- **The build.** The Home is rendered per request in one pass, so the build prerenders nothing of it: its manifest entry is empty and blocking (`htmlSize` 0), `index.html` is empty, and its segment files hold only the framework's generic not-found fallback (no Home or journal text, no album address). Before the no-JavaScript fix, `index.html` held the generic shell (the frame, the drawing, the introduction).
- **Transitions,** each seen on the next request: unpublishing, publishing again, deleting, restoring (still absent), publishing again, and changing an address (the new one listed, the old one never).

## The client router

The Home makes the first public links into `/albums/*`, which M3.1 left to be measured ([M3.1](m3-1.md#g2-and-the-cache-hit-marker)). In Chromium, on the production build, at 1440 and 390 px (`e2e/home.spec.ts`):

- A push navigation to the Home after a journal is unpublished reads the lists again: the journal is gone. The lists are dynamic (`staleTimes.dynamic` is 0); only the shell, which holds no data, is reused for up to 300 s.
- A Home loaded before the unpublish still shows the link; following it shows the not-found view, never the journal.
- **Going back** to the journal's page after it was unpublished shows it again ("Pink Moon"), from the browser's own history of what this reader was already sent; a reload shows the not-found view. That is not a disclosure (nothing new reaches the reader), but it is not "the next request" in G2's words either: recorded for the owner under G2, with any hosted CDN's behavior.
- **Not tested:** a not-found prefetched for an address in the short window between an unpublish and a republish could be reused for up to 300 s, hiding a journal that is public again. The Home links only addresses that were served when it rendered, so this needs the journal to be unpublished before the link is prefetched and republished before it is followed. It can only hide, never reveal; recorded under G2.

## Rendering, JavaScript, and layout shift

**How it was, and why.** Under Cache Components, a page whose data is read behind Suspense is sent as a prerendered shell, and the data streams afterwards in hidden markup (`<div hidden id="S:n">` at the end of the body) that React's inline script moves into place. The first version of the Home did that, in two parts; its footer, painted under the introduction, was pushed down when the lists arrived, a layout shift of **0.079** at 1440 px in one of three loads. The second version made the lists one part and hid the footer until it arrived, which removed the shift but meant that **without JavaScript neither the lists nor the footer showed**: the reveal is a script. The owner did not accept that.

**Now.** The Home renders on the server per request in one pass and is sent complete. `export const instant = false` is the framework's documented way to let a route block instead of prerendering a shell (`node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/03-api-reference/03-file-conventions/02-route-segment-config/instant.md`, "Disabling static shell validation"; the build's "blocking route" error otherwise). The read is no longer behind Suspense, so the page waits for it (one small query) and its lists are in the HTML. Nothing else changed: the switch is checked first, the read is the reading entry point's, the response is `no-store`, nothing is cached, and the build now prerenders nothing of the Home. The footer rule is gone.

- **Without JavaScript,** at 1440 and 390 px, with reads on: the drawing, "Most recent", both sections with every seeded journal visible, links that load the journal's address, and the footer, with nothing left in hidden markup; with reads off, "Nothing published yet." and the footer. The shared header and footer show on the album journal and the not-found view too. These tests failed against the streamed version (checked by building it again).
- **Layout shift:** 0, measured, since nothing arrives after the page.
- **The cost:** the first byte waits for the Home's query. Locally that is a few milliseconds; on a hosted database it adds its round trip.
- **Two side effects,** found by the focused review: a client navigation to `/` (from an album journal or the not-found view) now shows nothing new until the server has answered, because the prefetched segments no longer carry the drawing and the introduction; and an error outside the read's `try` now happens before any byte is sent, so it would be the framework's error page instead of a page that had already started. Neither discloses anything.
- **The album journal** (M3.1, unchanged) still streams its content after a shell, so without JavaScript it shows its header, its footer, and "Loading…". Making it render in one pass would change M3.1's approved route and its evidence (A2), so it is recorded for the owner, not done here; it is tracked in issue #44, to be resolved before any hosted launch.
- The two browser tests that failed intermittently in the streamed version read the page before its parts were revealed; they passed 48 of 48 repeated runs then, and the Home no longer streams.

## Responsive and accessible

- **Widths:** 320, 390, 640, 768, 960, 961, 1024, 1100, 1101, 1280, 1440, and 2560 px: no sideways scroll, the shell `min(100% − 35.2px, 1320px)`, the drawing centered, the margin column beside the lists above 720 px and over them below, nothing in a card or a row past its box. At 768 px four albums were about 90 px wide and a rating ran into the next card; albums are now three to a row from 961 to 1100 px and two from 721 to 960 px (recorded in the design system), and a rating line wraps inside its card.
- **Zoom:** every size is in px, so a browser's text-size setting does not enlarge the page, and browser zoom does. 200% zoom of a 1280 px window lays the page out at 640 px, which is tested. (A first version "tested 200% text" by enlarging the root font size, which enlarged nothing; the accessibility review caught it, and the claim was removed.)
- **Accessibility:** axe (WCAG 2.2 A and AA) with no violation, populated and switched off; every link at least 44px, the newest journal's link also with a one-letter title, and wrapping a very long one-word title at 320 px; a row's link names its title, then its kind, then its date; the skip link first, then focus on `<main>`, then the newest journal, every row, and every card, each with the 2px ink ring; `lang="en"`; reduced motion (no transition); forced colors (the drawing's white ground, real borders on frames and rules, an underline on the current page).
- **Typography:** only Kalam and Alegreya Sans render; the section titles Kalam 700 at 27px; the introduction 23px (20.5px); titles 22px (20px for album cards on a phone); the drawing 380px (300px).
