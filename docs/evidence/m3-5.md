# Evidence: M3.5, public artist pages with photographs

## Scope

Authorized by the owner (issue #45). The public artist page at `/artists/<address>`, in the approved V4.4 `Artist` layout, showing the photograph the author chose in the studio (M3.3) with its attribution, and the album journals and track reflections about the artist's music that are already public. **Local implementation and review only:** nothing is deployed, public reads are never enabled on a reachable host, gates G1 to G8 stay open, issue #44 stays open, and no migration, backup, or development database was touched.

## Public artist visibility

**An artist is public only while a served album journal is about an album that credits it, and the artist is not archived** (`publicArtists` in [`queries.ts`](../../src/modules/publication/queries.ts)). "Served" is the rule the sitemap and the Home already use (`servedAlbumJournalAddresses`): the journal's current slug was published, on an entry with a publicly visible language version, so the rule is built from the publication predicate and never re-implemented.

Never public because of:

- the catalog record alone, or a credit on an album that has no journal;
- a photograph (every artist in the oracle test has one);
- an artist journal: artist journals have no public reading contract, so their text, their existence, and their publication state are not read at all;
- draft, private, deleted, or restored writing (a restored journal comes back private, ADR 0005), or a translation that is not itself published;
- an archived artist, whose credit stays plain text on the album journal (Product, rule 6).

A published translation is enough by itself, as for any public read (ADR 0002, D3.2).

Credits on tracks (`track_artists`) do not make an artist public: the page is about the albums an artist is credited on, which is what the album journal's header shows.

## Addresses

Artists have no stored slug, and adding one is a migration the owner has not authorized. The address is derived from the name with the entry slugs' own normalization ([`artist-address.ts`](../../src/modules/publication/artist-address.ts)), **over the visible artists only**:

- a unique name is the bare name (`/artists/jeff-buckley`);
- visible artists that share a name each get `<name>-<suffix>`, ten hexadecimal digits of a SHA-256 of a fixed purpose string and the artist's id: stable, opaque, and no identifier; while two share a name, neither has the bare name, so a link can never reach the wrong one;
- a name with nothing usable in it is `artist`, with the same rules;
- an address that two artists would still share is served for neither.

Because only visible artists are considered, a catalog-only or private namesake can never show through, not even as a suffix (tested). There is no redirect, because nothing records a previous address.

**The cost, for the owner:** an address changes when the name changes in the catalog, or when a second visible artist of the same name appears or disappears, and the old address then answers the unknown-address `404`. The internal links (album journals, the sitemap) are computed per request and never break; external links can. A stored, retained artist slug (like `entry_slugs`, with `308` redirects) would remove that, and is the proposed follow-up migration.

## The page

The approved `Artist` screen is an artist *journal*. With no public contract for artist journals, the page shows the name, the photograph, and "In this journal", and nothing invented: no facts line, no biography, no opening sentence or essay, no dates, no "Memories with his music: None yet.", no "All artists" ([Design System § Public artist page](../design-system.md#public-artist-page-m35) lists every difference). "Album journals" shows each album once, by its presented journal (ADR 0002, D1.2), with "<year> · <written> / <total> tracks"; "Tracks I wrote about" lists the tracks whose reflections are public in the version a reader is shown, linked to the album journal's tracks.

It is rendered on the server per request in one pass (`instant = false`, as the Home is), so it reads the same without JavaScript. The build prerenders nothing of it: its `.html` is empty and its segment files hold only the generic not-found fallback inside the artist layout (no artist data), the same class of artifact [ADR 0006 A2](../decisions/0006-publication-decision-and-acknowledgement.md#a2-the-frameworks-cache-marker-and-the-generic-prerendered-shell) allows for the Home.

## Photograph integration and attribution

- **The selected photograph, from stored provenance.** The read model parses the seven stored columns with M3.3's strict `artistPhotoOf`: the image and page addresses are rebuilt from the file name, the license must be one of Refrain's table, the credit must be plain text, and the provenance (SHA-1, upload time, approval time) must be present. Any other value shows the fallback, and never hides the artist (six tampered forms tested). Nothing is chosen automatically, no provider is called, and no binary is copied: the reader's browser loads Wikimedia's own thumbnail (330 and 500 px, `srcset`).
- **Attribution in the HTML.** The caption "Photo: [credit] · [license, linked to its deed] · via Wikimedia Commons", and a native **Photo details** disclosure with the work's title linked to its file on Commons, the credit, the source, the license (linked, with "Adaptations must use the same license." for CC BY-SA), and the changes ("Resized, and cropped to fit the frame. No other changes."). This is everything TASL (title, author, source, license) and the CC 4.0 "indicate changes" clause ask for, and it is what the stored provenance can support accurately. Public-domain marks show the name "(as marked on Wikimedia Commons)" with no link, as in the studio.
- **Framing.** The 4:5 frame with `object-fit: cover`; the crop is the frame's and is stated as a change. Nothing else is altered.
- **Unavailable thumbnails.** The image is removed when it fails, so only the fallback shows, and the frame's accessible name and words say "not available just now"; without JavaScript a failed image with an empty `alt` draws nothing. Tested with a thumbnail that answers `404`.
- **No Open Graph image.** A social preview would copy the photograph to another service's cache, outside its attribution.

## License and rights handling

The owner approves using appropriately licensed Commons photographs; each file's license still applies, and **nothing here asserts that every Commons photograph is cleared for every use.** The known limitations stay explicit and open for the hosted review ([Security § Deployment gate](../security.md#deployment-gate), item 7):

- **Several licenses.** Commons reports one license per file; a page that lists more than one (alternatives, or cumulative conditions) is not detected.
- **The description page can change.** The license, credit, and warnings live in the description page, whose revision is not stored (the deferred migration of M3.3). The studio's check compares the file and its metadata with Commons; the public page shows what was approved.
- **Public domain depends on the country**, and the site's country is not decided.
- **Personality, privacy, and publicity rights** of the people shown are not covered by a copyright license.
- **Problem tags** (no permission, no source, a failed license review) are not checked.
- **Removal or correction requests** from Commons, a rights holder, or a person shown: below.

## Removal and fallback

**Actionable now:** in the studio, the artist's photograph page removes the photograph at once, after a confirmation, also while the artist's page is public, for an archived artist, and while Commons is unreachable; the next request shows the empty frame (HTTP test). The writing and its publication are untouched (row checksum). **Before hosting**, the owner records the contact channel for requests and how quickly they are met; the procedure itself is in [Security § Deployment gate](../security.md#deployment-gate), item 7.

## Publication and concurrency

**An artist page opens only through a confirmed publication.** This is [ADR 0006 amendment A5](../decisions/0006-publication-decision-and-acknowledgement.md#a5-artist-pages-open-only-through-a-confirmed-publication), **approved by the owner on 2026-11-03**; it records the full rule, its effect on §§ 1 to 3 and § 7 of the accepted text, the lock order, and the residual windows, which the approval leaves open. In short:

- **The publication review discloses and binds the pages it opens** (the owner's decision in the review of PR #46, which narrows § 2's "catalog metadata is not in the fingerprint" for artist pages only): the artist pages publishing would open, with name, address, and photograph, and the public pages whose address a namesake would change. The publish recomputes them under the entry lock; any change is `confirmation_stale`, with the fresh disclosure and no token.
- **Refused** (`public_page`): a photograph set or replaced while the artist has a page; a credit change that would give an artist a page for the first time; restoring an archived artist when it would give it a page, with or without a photograph.
- **Serialized, never refused:** renaming and archiving an artist.
- **Always allowed:** removing a photograph (owner decision R4; the takedown path). This differs on purpose from the artwork commands, which refuse a removal too.
- **Lock order** in every one of them: the album-journal entries that decide, `FOR SHARE` in ascending id order, then the catalog rows, then the decision, then the write. A publication's `FOR UPDATE` on an entry either waits for the command, or the command waits for it and sees it.
- **The studio says so:** the photograph page and its review show "Shown on a public page" and offer no approval; the publication review lists "Artist pages that will open".

### How each exposure-changing operation is handled (audit, the owner's item 4)

| Operation | Before this round | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rename a publicly visible artist | Allowed, no lock: name and address change at once | Allowed (catalog wording, C11/G8), serialized with the publications of journals crediting that artist, and a review that disclosed the old name or address goes stale. The old address answers the unknown-address `404` (no stored slug) |
| Archive a publicly visible artist | Allowed, no lock | Allowed (a reduction), serialized the same way; its effect on a namesake's address is a residual window |
| Remove a photograph | Allowed | Allowed: the takedown path, also while public, for an archived artist, while Commons is down; the next request shows the empty frame |
| Set or replace a photograph | Refused while public | Unchanged; also stale for a review that disclosed the page |
| Add an album credit | Allowed, no lock: could open a page silently | **Refused** when it would open a page; allowed otherwise |
| Remove or reorder album credits, change `credited_as` | Allowed | Allowed: removal reduces; wording is C11/G8 |
| Restore an archived artist | Refused only with a photograph | **Refused** whenever it would open a page |
| Publish an album journal | Opened artist pages without listing them | Lists and binds them; stale on any change |
| Unpublish, delete an album journal | Allowed (reduction) | Unchanged |

### Remaining publication risks (recorded in A5, not closed)

Each is in A5's "Residual windows" with its exact conditions. None opens a page for an artist that no review disclosed and that had no page; each needs the author's own concurrent requests within milliseconds, or ends as if two allowed changes had run one after the other:

- a journal created, or a credit added on another album, inside a command's window, then published before it commits;
- a publication racing the unpublication or deletion of another journal that gave the same artist its page, or that credit's removal: the page can disappear for a moment and come back through a journal whose review did not list it;
- a namesake archived, renamed, uncredited, or given a page during a publication: the opening page's address can end up the other one;
- a deadlock of three concurrent requests (availability only: one is aborted, nothing is written);
- renames and credit wording changing public pages without a confirmation (C11, G8), and addresses that change without a redirect (no stored slug).

## Image privacy and the CSP

- Every response under `/artists/` carries the public policy plus exactly `https://thumb.wikimedia.org`; the Home, the album journal, the sitemap, and `robots.txt` do not allow it. The page and the unknown-address `404` under `/artists/` carry the same policy, so they cannot be told apart.
- The image is requested with `referrerpolicy="no-referrer"`, and the outbound links with `rel="noreferrer"`; the page's own `Referrer-Policy` is `strict-origin-when-cross-origin`.
- **A referrer policy does not stop the contact itself:** loading the photograph tells Wikimedia's servers the reader's IP address, user agent, and which file is shown (never the page). This belongs in the privacy notice before hosting.
- **Direct loading versus a proxy.** Direct loading needs no infrastructure, copies no binary (as the task asks), and uses Wikimedia's own CDN; its costs are the third-party contact above and a dependency on Wikimedia's availability, which the fallback absorbs. A proxy would hide the reader from Wikimedia, but would fetch arbitrary-looking addresses from the server (an SSRF surface to constrain), copy and serve the images (bandwidth, and a cache that ADR 0006 § 11 says needs its own ADR), and add a route to every gate. **A proxy is not required** for the local milestone; whether to want one is an owner decision before hosting, recorded with the privacy notice.
