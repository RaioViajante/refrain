# Evidence: M3.8a, public archives and navigation

Authorized by the owner on 2026-11-04 after the M3.8 discovery report (issue #55). M3.8 is split into two increments, each reviewed and merged on its own: **M3.8a**, this one, adds the three public archives and the four-section navigation; **M3.8b**, the Home's expansion, is not authorized. **Local only**: nothing is deployed, `REFRAIN_PUBLIC_READS_ENABLED` is never enabled on a reachable host, and gates G1 to G8 stay open (#47).

No migration, no new dependency, no new visibility rule, and no change to any ADR: ADR 0006 D4 applies to the archives as written (A4 covers `/` alone and is unchanged), and A6, proposed for M3.8b, is not drafted here.

## What was built

| Surface | What it lists | Route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `/albums` | Each album that has a served album journal, once. Its card opens the journal ADR 0002 presents for the album (the featured one if it is visible, otherwise the most recently published), with the cover through the existing artwork reader, the title, "<artists> · <year>", "<n> of <N> tracks written", and the rating when one is set. **Every other served journal about the album** is a quiet link under the card, "Another journal, published <date>", so no published journal becomes reachable only through the Home's five | `src/app/(archives)/albums/` |
| `/artists` | Exactly the artists whose page is served (ADR 0006 A5: credited on an album with a served journal, not archived), at the addresses their pages answer at, alphabetically; "1 album", or "1 album · 2 journals" when there are more journals than albums. No photograph, no artist kind | `src/app/(archives)/artists/` |
| `/memories` | Every memory whose address is served, the newest publication of the version shown first (ties by address): its "when" as written (never read as a date), the title, an excerpt, the music line, and "Published <date>" | `src/app/(archives)/memories/` |
| The header | Home, Albums, Artists, Memories, always, in every state of the switch. On a section's own page the link is `aria-current="page"`; on a page inside it (an album journal, an artist, a memory) `aria-current="true"`; both have the drawn underline | [`public-frame.tsx`](../../src/app/(public)/_components/public-frame.tsx) |
| The sitemap | The three archives, beside the Home and every served address, while reads are on | [`sitemap.xml/route.ts`](../../src/app/sitemap.xml/route.ts) |

The read models are Publication's ([`public-archives.ts`](../../src/modules/publication/public-archives.ts)), built only from the existing fragments: `servedAlbumJournalAddresses`, `servedMemoryAddresses`, `presentedJournals`, `publicArtists`, `publicLanguageVersions`, `publicTrackReflectionTexts`, and `versionPreference`; artist addresses come from the same `artistAddresses` rule as every artist page, over that one statement's rows, which are the whole visible set. They are exported only through the reading entry point ([`public.ts`](../../src/modules/publication/public.ts)), each behind the switch. They return no entry or catalog identifier, no status, no deletion state, no unpublished language, and no writing beyond a memory's title and excerpt.

**The excerpt** ([`excerpt.ts`](../../src/modules/publication/excerpt.ts)) is cut on the server, so a list page never carries a memory's whole writing, and the query reads only each memory's first 16 top-level blocks: the first paragraph with readable text among them (a quoted one counts; a heading is used only when there is no paragraph), read with the public renderer's restrictions (a `doc` only, text and hard breaks only, marks ignored, nesting deeper than 8 not read, never more loosely than the renderer counts it, so an excerpt never holds text the memory's page would not show; collection stopped long before a huge paragraph's or text node's end), white space and control, separator, and bidirectional characters made one space, invisible marks inside a word (soft hyphen, zero-width characters, directional marks) removed, then cut to about 240 characters at a word boundary, never inside a character (graphemes), with no trailing comma, dash, or opening bracket or quote (a closing quote stays), and "…" only when that paragraph itself was cut.

## Routing and the framework's request forms (the spike)

The archives are one-segment routes, so the spike measured, on the production build, every request form with the switch off and on before any assertion was written:

- The proxy matchers `/albums`, `/artists`, and `/memories` reach every transport form: the router's `_rsc`, prefetch, and segment-prefetch requests, `.rsc`, `.json`, `/_next/data/<build>/<archive>.json`, and the **segment files of a one-segment route, which sit beside it** (`/albums.segments/_tree.segment.rsc`), not under it. A first version added explicit `.segments` matchers; a mutation that removed them changed nothing, because the framework already extends `/albums` to those forms, so they were removed and the HTTP tests pin the behaviour instead.
- With the switch off, `routePublicArchiveRequest` (switch, then method, before any read) answers the unknown-address `404` for every method and form.
- With the switch on, the page renders per request (`instant = false`), so the framework serves no segment files for it: they, `.rsc`, and `.json` get its generic, data-free `404`, the same with the switch on or off.
- **A percent-encoded archive** (`/%61lbums`) matches no route, so the framework would answer it with its static `404` and `Cache-Control: s-maxage=31536000`, a cacheable response the proxy would have let through. The proxy now serves an archive only at its own spelling, and gives an encoded one the unknown address's `no-store` answer, with the switch on or off. (The same framework behaviour exists for any encoded spelling of a route that has a prerendered shell, for example `/st%75dio`, before this milestone; recorded under G2.)
- The trailing slash is the existing `no-store` `308` to the archive.
- The build prerenders nothing of an archive: an empty, blocking entry, as for the Home, whose segment and postponed-state files hold the generic frame and not-found fallback only (A2).

## Discoverability

Every served album journal is reachable from `/albums`: the integration test asserts, over every state of a journal, that the set of addresses the archive links (cards and the links under them) equals the sitemap's set, with no duplicate; a test publishes eight journals so that the Home's five cannot hold them all, and the archive still lists all eight. An album appears once even with three served journals (and a draft, a private, and a deleted one, which never appear); when the featured journal is unpublished, the newest remaining one becomes the card.

## Tests

All against the disposable test database and synthetic `[Test]` data.

- **Unit:** the excerpt (short, empty paragraphs, marks and hard breaks, a heading before a paragraph, a quoted paragraph, a long cut at a word boundary, no trailing comma, a very long word, flags and combining marks never split, controls and bidirectional characters, malformed and hostile documents, depth, a huge paragraph); the archive decision for every switch value and method; the count and music lines; the reading entry point answers empty archives with the switch off before it touches a database; the static scans of the public surface and the studio, extended to the new route group, reads, exports, matcher, and routing call.
- **PostgreSQL** ([`public-archives.integration.test.ts`](../../src/modules/publication/public-archives.integration.test.ts)): the Albums and Artists archives against an independent oracle over 168 states of a journal (deleted, the English and the Portuguese status, the original, the reflection's flag and text), each with its own artist and one artist shared by all, checking presence, the version's date, the rating, both track counts, and the artists' counts; the linked set equals the sitemap's; the artists equal the artist pages; several journals about one album, featured and not; the Home's limit; unpublished, re-addressed, deleted, and restored journals; the count of reflections (not included, other language, reference edition only); the projections' keys. Artists hidden for every reason (catalog-only, draft-only, private-only, deleted-only, archived, memory-only, artist-journal-only, photograph-only); namesakes, also beside a hidden namesake; alphabetical order. Memories against the predicate's oracle over every state with one and two languages, with the chosen version's title, excerpt, and date; order and ties; unpublished, deleted then restored, re-addressed; the music named as the memory page names it; a blank "when"; no identifier and no whole writing.
- **Mutations of the read models:** removing the version-shown join's predicate (albums, memories), removing it together with the served-address rule, listing every catalog artist credited anywhere, dropping the other journals, and counting every flagged reflection each failed the integration tests. Removing the served-address rule alone was not detected, and is equivalent: the version-shown join reads only `publicLanguageVersions`, so an entry with no visible version drops out either way.
- **HTTP** ([`public-archives.http.test.ts`](../../src/testing/public-archives.http.test.ts)), five servers of one build (the switch `true`, unset, `TRUE`, `1`, `true `): with the switch off, each archive in 22 request forms (six methods, RSC, prefetch, three segment prefetches, three segment files, `.rsc`, `.json`, `/_next/data`, a bot, `Accept`, `If-None-Match`, `Range`, percent-encoded) is identical in status, headers, and body to the same form of an unknown address in its section; with it on, each lists exactly what was seeded as public, every link answers `200`, the earlier Grace journal is linked under its album, an unpublished journal disappears on the next request, the Artists list has no image and the artist pages' image policy, the Memories list carries the first paragraph only and its `lang`; no response is storable, and a page that lists anything never carries the framework's cache marker; the trailing-slash `308` and the `.html` and `.meta` names answer the same on or off; titles, canonical addresses, and `og:url`; the navigation's four links and current section on every page and with the switch off; the sitemap; and no response of the file named any hidden journal, artist, or memory. [`public-build-output.http.test.ts`](../../src/testing/public-build-output.http.test.ts) gains the archives' empty entries.
- **Mutations of the routing:** making the archive decision ignore the switch, and also removing the pages' own guards, each failed 60 of the switch-off cases.
- **Browser** ([`e2e/archives.spec.ts`](../../e2e/archives.spec.ts)), at 1440 px and at 390 px on a touch screen: each archive's composition, following an archive row to its page, the earlier journal's link, the navigation on the Home, a journal, an artist, a memory, and the not-found view; the switch off; reading without JavaScript; axe (WCAG 2.2 A and AA), the skip link, keyboard order, a 2px focus ring, 44px targets that nothing covers (cards, the other journals' links, rows, memory titles, the navigation), one `h1`, the writing's `lang`, reduced motion, forced colors, no layout shift; no sideways scroll and the four links whole **on one line** at 320, 360, 390, 768, 1280, 1440, and 2560 px.
- **Added after the reviews:** a card's rating and count from the journal it opens and never from a hidden featured one; an artist credited only on a recording; memories whose original is Portuguese; a memory unpublished between the archive's two statements (its songs are not read); a public reflection on a track outside the reference edition (not counted); the first readable paragraph after empty blocks; the excerpt's invisible marks, closing quotes, text of marks only, a non-`doc` body, and one huge text node; the router's RSC and segment-prefetch requests followed to the archive with the switch on (`200`, and whatever carries the framework's marker is data-free).
- **Existing tests changed by the new contract:** the header now has four links on the Home, album journal, artist, memory, and not-found pages (their browser and build-output tests), and `/memories` is now the archive, not a `404` (the memory page's HTTP test).

## Hosted-release requirements

Nothing here closes a gate; G1 to G8 stay open and #47 stays open.

- **G8 (C11), more surfaces.** The archives repeat catalog wording that a catalog edit changes without a confirmation: album titles and credits (`credited_as`), years, artist names (and with them their order and addresses), the reference edition's track count, and a memory's song titles and credited artists. No catalog field becomes public that was not public before: the artist kind and sort name stay private, and order is by the name shown.
- **G1:** the archives answer `404` while reads are off and `200` while they are on, and need the same hosted proof as every other route; name-derived artist addresses (A5's residual windows) appear in one more list.
- **G2:** the framework's static, cacheable `404` for a percent-encoded spelling of any route with a prerendered shell (`/st%75dio`) predates M3.8a; the archives are protected from it in the proxy, and the general case is recorded for the owner.
- **Deployment gate items 6 and 7:** covers appear on one more page; no photograph appears on any archive or on the Home.
- **Namesakes:** two visible artists with one name are two rows with one name, each at its own suffixed address, as on their pages; nothing tells a reader which is which (WCAG 2.4.4: identical link names to different pages). A stored artist slug, or a disambiguating fact, would need the owner's decision.
- **Security headers on the framework's transport `404`s:** with the switch on, `/albums.rsc` and `/albums.segments/…` get the framework's data-free `404` without the CSP and framing headers (the header rules match `/albums/:path*`), as `/index.rsc` does on `main`. Recorded under G5.
- **A second address for an archive:** `/_next/data/<build>/albums.json` answers the Albums archive while reads are on (`no-store`, the public policy). Recorded under G1 and G2 for the hosted checks.
- **The artist page's count** (M3.5) can exceed its total for a reflection on a track outside the reference edition; the archive's cannot. A follow-up for the artist page.
