# Evidence: M3.8b, the public Home's expansion

Authorized by the owner on 2026-11-04 after the M3.8b architecture proposal (issue #57), with [ADR 0006 amendment A6](../decisions/0006-publication-decision-and-acknowledgement.md#a6-the-public-home-also-shows-served-memories-and-public-artists) approved subject to two corrections, both applied: the excerpt guarantee (the server may read a published memory to derive its excerpt; only the bounded excerpt is returned or serialized) and the snapshot guarantee (one read-only `REPEATABLE READ` snapshot per response, with no promise about a publication that commits after it began). **Local only**: nothing is deployed, `REFRAIN_PUBLIC_READS_ENABLED` is never enabled on a reachable host, and gates G1 to G8 stay open (#47). Follow-ups #44, #50, #51, and #54 are unchanged.

No migration, no new dependency, no new visibility rule, and no change to the predicate, the writers' allowlist, the confirmations, A1 to A5, D4, the archives, the header, the character, or the introduction. The first commit records A6; the rest implement it.

## The snapshot (correction B)

- **Supported without architectural change.** Drizzle's node-postgres driver opens `begin isolation level repeatable read read only` from the transaction options, as the previews already used. Measured in [`public-home.integration.test.ts`](../../src/modules/publication/public-home.integration.test.ts): inside the snapshot, `transaction_isolation` is `repeatable read` and `transaction_read_only` is `on`, and a write fails with `25006`.
- **Consistency across sections.** Over two real connections, an unpublication is committed by the second connection right after the Home's first statement. The response still shows the memory in Recently written and in Memories, with its music, because every statement saw the same snapshot; the next request shows it nowhere. With the snapshot removed (mutation), the sections disagreed and the test failed.
- **The legitimate limit, as A6 states it:** a response reflects the state when its snapshot began; a publication or unpublication that commits during the request is seen from the next request. Each statement still applies the predicate itself.
- **No lock** is taken; the reading-file scan allows exactly one transaction call, in `snapshot.ts`, with literally `isolationLevel: "repeatable read"` and `accessMode: "read only"`, and probes show it still flags any other options, a spread, a variable, and the same call in any other reading file.

## The excerpt (correction A)

The Home's memories are the archive's cards, so the excerpt is the M3.8a rule unchanged: the query reads the first 16 top-level blocks of the version shown, and `excerpt.ts` returns the first readable paragraph, cut at about 240 characters. The read model returns only that excerpt, and every Home component is a server component that takes the Publication module's values as types only (a static check), so no document reaches a client component or the Flight payload. Tested: a second paragraph and an 18th block never appear in the read model, the HTML, or any RSC form; a body that is not a readable document gives no excerpt.

**Density on a phone (the owner's decision).** A full excerpt is three lines at 1440px, as the screen's "two or three lines at most", and was five at 390px. At 720px and below the Home now draws at most three lines, in CSS alone; the excerpt in the page is the server's, whole and unchanged, so a screen reader reads all of it once, and the title opens the whole memory. Tested at 320, 360, and 390px (and unchanged at 721 and 1440px) with the served excerpt, a 240-character word, and mixed scripts (combining accents, CJK, Cyrillic, Arabic, emoji): at most three drawn lines, the authored text unchanged in the DOM and whole in the accessibility tree, no sideways scroll, the same without JavaScript, and the archive's excerpt never clamped. Removing the rule failed the test. `visual-review/m3-8b/contact-excerpt-390.png` shows it beside the screen.

**What "equal" means here, precisely.** Three comparisons, and only the first two are byte-level:

1. **A switch-off server against itself across database states** (the A4 and A6 guarantee): the whole received response, status, every header, and the decoded body with its Flight data, is equal to what the same server answered with nothing published, with only the framework's per-request key masked (as A4's required tests specify). This holds in every form, in the empty, hidden-only, and populated databases, and after every transition.
2. **The switch-on server with only hidden writing against its own empty-site response**: the same, byte for byte apart from that key.
3. **A switch-off server against the switch-on empty site:** equal in status, every header, the page as a reader receives it, and the Flight rows as a sorted multiset; **not byte-identical.** The same stream is chunked and ordered differently (which `<script>` push carries a row, and the order of rows), because the switch-on server awaits its empty read and the switch-off one reads nothing.

The tests read each response whole, decoded by the HTTP client: they do not observe HTTP transfer framing (chunk sizes, timing), and nothing here claims anything about it. **Against the contract:** A4 requires that, while the switch is off, the Home answers "what a site with nothing published answers, in status, every header, the visible text, the markup, and the metadata", and that "its whole response is the same whatever the database holds". Comparison 1 establishes the second literally, and 1 and 3 together the first. A6, item 6, and its required tests compare with the empty-site baseline, which is comparison 1. No discrepancy was found that these tests cannot establish. Whether streaming timing reveals anything is a side-channel question outside this milestone.

**The excerpt's density at 390px: the owner's decision.** A full excerpt was five lines on a phone. By the owner's decision (review of #58 at de5bcb2), the Home draws at most three lines of a memory's excerpt at 720px and below (V4.4's phone breakpoint), in CSS only (`-webkit-line-clamp: 3` on `.ph-memories .px-excerpt`). Unchanged: the server's excerpt (one rule, shared with the archive), A6's disclosure, the desktop Home, and the Memories archive. See [The excerpt](#the-excerpt-correction-a).

## Hosted-release requirements

Nothing here closes a gate; G1 to G8 stay open and #47 stays open.

- **G8 (C11), more surfaces.** The Home now shows catalog wording that a catalog edit changes without a confirmation: artist names (and with them their addresses, their order among ties, and a namesake's suffix), the artists' served counts (a credit removed, or added for an artist who already has a page), and a memory's song titles and credited artists (an album link's title and credits), beside the album titles, years, and credits A4 already allowed. No catalog field became public that was not public before; the artist kind, sort name, and photograph stay off the Home. A memory's title, "when", writing, and song order are authored atoms bound by ADR 0006 § 1 and § 5, not C11.
- **G1:** name-derived artist addresses (A5's residual windows) now appear on the Home; a rename after a Home is sent makes its link answer the unknown-address `404` until the next request, as on the archive.
- **The snapshot's limit** is by design (A6, item 8), not a gate: a response reflects the state when its snapshot began.
- **Deployment gate items 6 and 7:** unchanged; covers as before, no photograph on the Home, and the Home's CSP has no new image host.
